E.A.T. Data Connections

How the Knowledge Graph, the Technical Services edge-notched cards, the Bibliography and the Datascape import join up in the Wikibase, where the joins are thin, and which of them would carry an exploration interface.

Second, the cross-project links are made by properties, not by shared membership. A card artist points at a Knowledge Graph artist through P278 proposed collaborator; a bibliography entry points at a Knowledge Graph press release through P247 bibliographic reference of; a card engineer's employer is a Knowledge Graph organization. Those bridges are what an interface can walk.

Figure 2. Every claim remembers the passage it came from

This is the property that makes the dataset unusual. 8,480 of the 16,422 statements on link-bearing items carry a reference block reference pointing to the text block that justifies them. The block has the passage, the passage has a parent document, the document has a scan. The same chain exists for cards. Roles sit in qualifiers on the same statements: object has role on 437 contributor claims, subject has role on 725 memberships and 394 contributed-to claims.

Where an interface would be interesting

Ranked by how much of the data each one actually exercises, and by whether the join it depends on is strong enough today.

1 · STRONGEST JOIN

The matchmaking file

Flip through 385 engineer cards with front and back scans. Each engineer has an employer, a street address, and fields of work; the back of the card names the artists they were proposed to. The 1,927 reciprocal proposed collaborator edges make a bipartite engineer–artist graph: Agnes Denes was proposed 25 engineers, Eugene Dolgoff 28 artists. Filter by field (laser, holography, ultrasonics), by employer (IBM, Grumman, Bell Labs), or by region (131 Manhattan, 68 New Jersey).

The question the data can almost answer: did any match happen? Only 48 card engineers exist in the Knowledge Graph and none shares a credited work with an artist they were paired with. That gap is itself a finding worth showing.

card ─P77→ engineer ─P278⇄ artist · engineer ─P110/P152/P202→ employer / address / field · card ─P200→ scans · every edge ─P26→ back-of-card block
2 · DENSEST SUBGRAPH

9 Evenings, night by night

Ten works, twenty dated performance instances between 13 and 23 October 1966 at the 69th Regiment Armory, 99 distinct contributors and 118 distinct devices, materials and phenomena. Contributors carry roles (performing artist 221, engineer 90, production team 29, performance engineer 27, choreographer, projectionist). The devices link upward to works and sideways to the engineers who built them. 96 archive documents and the 1966 press (101 references) have 9 Evenings as main subject.

A calendar strip across the top, one performance per cell; click a cell for its crew, its kit, the programme page that documents it, and the reviews that followed.

event ←P96─ performance ─P127→ work · performance ─P75→ person [P262 role] · performance ─P185→ device [P26 → block → programme scan] · reference ─P98→ date, ─P103→ publication
3 · UNIQUE TO THIS DATASET

Read the archive, with the graph underneath

470 documents are already split into blocks, with 4,716 mentions of 2,045 entities. Render each document as its passages, with every mentioned entity hot. Click a name and see the person; click the person and see every passage, across letters, press releases, programme and card backs, that mentions them. Reverse the provenance chain: highlight which sentence produced which claim.

This is the view that no other E.A.T. resource has, and it is 52% of all statements.

document ←P24─ block ─P19/P20→ text · block ─P21→ entity · statement ─P26→ block · document ─P200→ scan, ─P138→ collection, ─P248→ bibliography entry
4 · FOUR SOURCES, ONE PROFILE

Who's who, assembled

A person page that stitches the four projects: Knowledge Graph facts and roles, authored bibliography entries (71 authors are KG persons), the engineer's card if one exists, and a link out to the Datascape actor page through the P245 formatter URL, where related actors, places and the médialab network graph live. 355 persons have thumbnails, 431 have Wikidata QIDs for further pull-through.

The Datascape pointer is the only way to reach that site's project–actor–activity data; it was never imported, so 615 Knowledge Graph persons are bare stubs here.

5 · TIME

Archive against press, 1960–1972

Knowledge Graph dates cluster hard on 1966 (166 of 283). Bibliography dates run 1965–1969 with the peak in 1969 (157). Two lanes on one axis: what E.A.T. produced (performances, letters, press releases, photographs) and what the world printed about it (New York Times 27, The Nation 19, Village Voice 13, Dagens Nyheter, Bijutsu-Techo, IEEE Spectrum). 18 explicit links tie a press release to its bibliography entry.

kg item ─P98→ date · reference ─P98→ date, ─P103→ publication, ─P104→ place · document ─P248⇄P247─ reference
6 · PLACE

Where the engineers were

352 engineers have street addresses as typed on the cards (Bell Labs in Murray Hill and Berkeley Heights, IBM, Grumman on Long Island, NYU, Rutgers). Geocode them and the Technical Services Program becomes a map of the 1966 New York–New Jersey industrial research belt. Add the 300 places of publication from the bibliography for a second layer: New York 142, Los Angeles 17, Tokyo 16, Stockholm 14, San Francisco 13.

Only 19 coordinates exist on E.A.T. items today; addresses are free text and would need geocoding.

engineer ─P152→ address (text) · ─P110→ employer · reference ─P104→ city [P146 coordinates on some]
7 · A BRIDGE TO BUILD

Technology as the join

Works say what they use (199 targets: TEEM, transmitters, contact microphones, Plexiglass); engineers say their field of work (65 targets). Only nine terms are shared today: laser, film, neon, motor, sound, computers, television, transducer, circuit. Reconcile the two vocabularies and you can ask: which card engineers worked in the technologies 9 Evenings actually needed?

performance ─P185→ term ←P202─ engineer · term ─P55→ class (subclass hierarchy is sparse)

Gaps that limit the joins

  • The card population is unreconciled. 670 of 688 card persons exist only in the card project. 306 of the 350 artists on card backs have no Knowledge Graph counterpart, and only 30 of them carry an occupation. Reconciling artists to the Knowledge Graph and Datascape persons is the single highest-value cleanup for ideas 1 and 4.
  • Datascape stubs are hollow. All 738 items are also Knowledge Graph items, but 615 persons carry only name, occupation and page ID. The site's own activities, project links and places were not imported, and its actor timeline looks broken (dates in 2010–2102). Either import it or treat P245 as a link-out.
  • Bibliography rarely points at works. Only 2 references have a main subject and 18 link to a document. Dates and venues are complete, so the time view works, but "reviews of this performance" needs subject tagging.
  • Duplicates. Seven Wikidata QIDs sit on two local items each (Drexel University twice, Design Research twice, Plexiglass/Lucite, National Endowment for the Arts twice, Black Mountain College twice, computer science in both projects).
  • Isolated persons. 387 persons have no item links in either direction; 568 have no outgoing links. Most are Datascape stubs and card artists.
  • Places are mostly free text. Street addresses are statement text; coordinates exist on 274 items in the Wikibase, but only 19 of them belong to E.A.T. projects.

What was downloaded

Everything is in the project folder, pulled with the semlab-data-process user agent.

  • data/items.json · 5,773 items with labels, descriptions, project tags and all direct claims (43,500 claims, objects labelled).
  • data/entities_full.json · full Wikibase JSON with qualifiers and references for the 1,737 items that carry link properties.
  • data/properties.json · all 280-odd property labels, descriptions and datatypes. data/external_items.json · the 279 items linked from E.A.T. items but belonging to other projects (Linked Jazz places, occupations, roles).
  • q.py runs a SPARQL query with the prefixes set; dump.py, fetch_entities.py, analyze.py, analyze2.py reproduce every number on this page.

Statement-level SPARQL (qualifiers, references) times out on query.semlab.io for whole-project scans; the Wikibase API wbgetentities in batches of 50 was fast and complete.
